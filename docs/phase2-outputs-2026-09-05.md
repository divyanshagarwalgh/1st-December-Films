## The read


A thirty second domestic story that turns a squeeze of ketchup into a note passed between a mother and a son, with the reply written in the same medium at the end. The device is a two-way gesture: an accidental heart in the tiffin compartment that the child answers, smudged, inside the lid. Everything rests on that final reveal reading instantly and reading as the boy's handwriting, not as a repeat of the opening shot, and on the ketchup itself being beautiful enough at macro to carry a product film without a single product claim.

## Beat sheet

- Dawn kitchen, rain outside, mother packs two tiffins while her son waits on the counter.
- The boy compares his tiffin to a classmate's; she deflects in one line.
- Parathas go in, ketchup goes in, the spiral lands as a heart by accident.
- She sees it, wipes the nozzle, leaves it.
- School gate in rain, umbrellas, the boy runs in without looking back.
- Classroom lunch, forty lids open at once.
- He opens his tiffin, the heart is there, two boys tease him.
- He shrugs, dips, eats, hides a smile.
- Evening kitchen, she washes the tiffin and finds a heart drawn back in the lid.
- Super, pack shot, voice over.

## Runtime and format


Three lines of dialogue and one voice over, ten beats, four scene locations. That is a lot of geography for thirty seconds: the school gate is one shot, the classroom is three, so the film runs at roughly two to three seconds a cut with the morning kitchen and the final reveal given the only breathing room. Thirty seconds is achievable but only if the gate and the teasing beat are treated as single frames. The fifteen second television cut will almost certainly drop the classmates entirely and run kitchen, tiffin, reveal. The six second digital bumper is a macro squeeze and the heart, which means the ketchup pour needs to be shot as its own unit at high frame rate, not lifted from coverage. Format: TVC with two cutdowns, family FMCG.

## Production breakdown


Locations: three, with the kitchen doubling for morning and evening. A real flat kitchen, a school gate exterior, a classroom. Cast: two principals, two featured boys, around forty child background artistes plus parents and umbrella crowd at the gate. Day throughout, with a pre-dawn look for the opening and a dusk look for the close, both of which will be lit rather than found. Flags: kids, crowd, water, food, minor VFX for ketchup continuity and nozzle cleanup. No stunts, no animals, no celebrity, no period, no vehicles.

**Complexity: complex.** Forty children in a classroom with a working lunch bell, wet-weather work at an exterior, and hero food craft on a product that changes shape on every take, all inside one schedule.

## Three comparable films

- [India Gate (2020)](https://1stdecember.com/work/india-gate): warm domestic footage married to controlled tabletop food photography and carried by a single voice over line, the same split between observed life and hero product craft this script needs.
- [Nestle Nangrow (2020)](https://1stdecember.com/work/nestl-nangrow): child casting at volume with chaperones and very short usable windows per child, and a montage held together by music rather than dialogue.
- [ZEE X FIFA (2026)](https://1stdecember.com/work/zee-x-fifa): around forty performers including children, all reacting on cue in built interiors, which is the classroom problem here in a different costume.

## Two directors

- [Shivang Monga](https://1stdecember.com/director/shivang-monga): everyday domestic comedy played straight, with non-actors and children carrying the beats rather than the writing, as in [Cars24, Family Promises: Sister Promise (2026)](https://1stdecember.com/work/cars24-sister-promise), and food ritual and brand memory handled without a demo in [Britannia, Good Day: Chai’s Better Half (2025)](https://1stdecember.com/work/britannia-good-day).
- [Atul Kattukaran](https://1stdecember.com/director/atul-kattukaran): strong on marrying real household footage with appetite-led tabletop passes, shown in [India Gate (2020)](https://1stdecember.com/work/india-gate), and on getting usable behaviour from young children inside a tight cut in [Nestle Nangrow (2020)](https://1stdecember.com/work/nestl-nangrow).

## What we would push on

- Rain. "Actual rain if the shoot lands in monsoon" is not a plan. Monsoon means lost days, wet kit, unusable sound and a school location you cannot hold. Budget the rain machine and the wet-down as the base case, and treat real rain as a bonus on the gate exterior only.
- The final heart. Right now nobody is told who drew it or when. If the audience thinks it is the same heart smudged, the film has no ending. That reveal needs a different shape, a different smear, and probably a beat earlier in the classroom where we see him do it. That costs a shot we do not have.
- The ketchup has to behave. A perfect spiral heart that reads at macro, holds for a dozen takes, does not slump under lights and matches across two scenes is a food styling and product specialist job with a dedicated table top session, plus a CG cleanup pass on the nozzle and the drip.
- Forty children.
