TITLE: EVERY BALL
Brand: a sportswear brand (cricket boots and kit)
Format: 90 second brand film, 30 second cut for TV

OPEN ON a hand gripping a worn cricket ball. Seam under two fingers. We never see a face for the first ten seconds.

VO (young, male, unpolished): You do not get a stadium. You get a strip of mud behind the water tank.

CUT TO a run-up. Not one boy. Every frame is a different boy, a different ground, a different city, but the run-up continues as if it were one delivery. A rooftop in Kanpur. A beach in Kochi. A school ground in Ranchi with a goat on the boundary. The camera is always at the same height and the same angle so the stitching feels like one motion.

VO: Nobody filmed your first wicket.

The leap. The arm comes over. A girl now, in a hijab, in Hyderabad. Then a boy in Shillong in the rain. The ball leaves the hand.

SFX: bat on ball, from somewhere off screen. Then nothing.

CUT TO the stump, cartwheeling, in slow motion, across six different grounds in six cuts.

SUPER: Every ball. Every yard.

VO: We saw it.

CUT TO wide. Hundreds of kids across all the grounds we have visited, now in one frame by composite, walking back to their marks.

END CARD: Brand logo. The new boot on a mud strip, not a studio floor.

Production notes: we want to cast real players, not actors. Ideally we source footage from kids themselves for part of the run-up montage and intercut with our own shoot. Minimum eight cities. The composite crowd shot at the end can be VFX.
